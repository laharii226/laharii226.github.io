Von Mises contour: the single equivalent stress that combines bending, shear and axial load, σvm = √(σ² + 3τ²), shown as a share of the material limit (blue = low, red = at the limit, purple = beyond it). Crowns are highest; mid-strut keeps a small share from shear instead of dropping to zero.

Stent model: each ring is a zigzag of beams joined at crowns. Squeeze: F = p·2πR·h / 2N per crown, M = (F/2)·L·cos θ, and the peak stress is σ = 6M / (t·w²), so thinner struts are far weaker. Crimp: the bending strain is ε ≈ 3·w·Δ / L². The bending moment is largest at the crowns and zero mid-strut, so colors fade along each strut. Steel and cobalt-chromium yield above about 0.15 to 0.2% strain and keep a permanent set, while nitinol recovers strains up to about 6%. Mesh motion is exaggerated in the squeeze case. Material values are simplified and illustrative.

Meaning

Models: for a bulge, hoop stress σ = P·r/t with t = r₀t₀/r, and a covered stent keeps the wall share Eₐt / (Eₐt + Eₛtₛ). For a narrowing, the stent pushes outward against the plaque like two springs in opposition, so the lumen opens by a fraction that grows with the stent's radial strength. Ratings, weights and stiffness values are illustrative teaching numbers, not clinical advice. Real choices depend on many more factors, and the brain case is drawn at the same scale for simplicity.
